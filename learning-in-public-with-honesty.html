Learning in Public Without Pretending to Have All the Answers

Sharing what I learn can create accountability, but only if I am clear about the difference between experience, opinion, and fact.

By Aviral Rajput · Published 2026-10-11 · 4 minute read

The value of documenting

Writing helps me organise ideas and notice what I do not yet understand. A public learning journal can also help others who are at a similar stage, especially when the lessons are specific and honest.

Avoiding the expert trap

I am a learner, not an authority on every topic I discuss. I want to explain ideas in plain language, mention uncertainty when it exists, and keep improving an article when I learn something new.

Useful writing

The most helpful posts often explain a problem, describe an approach, and reflect on what worked or did not. They do not need inflated claims or a dramatic personal brand to be worthwhile.

An ongoing commitment

This website will change as I learn. I hope it becomes a record of curiosity, practice, and thoughtful work over time.

This article reflects my current experience and perspective as a student. It is a learning journal, not professional advice.
